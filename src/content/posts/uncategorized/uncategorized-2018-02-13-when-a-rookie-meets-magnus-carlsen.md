When you think you have a million-dollar idea and meet someone who has already implemented one that is many times better than the one you originally thought about, it's heartbreaking. I faced a similar situation at the India Electronics Week 2018.

As I was relentlessly going through each and every stall that I believed mattered, I came across this one overcrowded one and decided to wait there till I got a good view.

![y5htpjtl](@assets/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/y5htpjtl.jpg)Their logo and banners suggested they dealt with something liquid, and I had a water filtration solution in mind. After recently getting to know about the benefits of [Kangen Water](https://www.enagic.com/?c=water-usage) and the probable death of other water filtration techniques I decided it was not worth the time.

Just when I was about to divert to a new exhibit, a product flyer caught my attention.

![WP_20180209_16_39_33_Pro.jpg](@assets/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/wp_20180209_16_39_33_pro.jpg)**I stood there for a couple of minutes, reading the same things over and over again...not able to comprehend this level of coincidence**. Slowly it struck me: This was probably something the latent entrepreneur in me never wanted to see.

A device that has the capabilities of a multimeter and an oscilloscope, has a clean and intuitive interface and above all, is real-time. One couldn't ask for anything more

\[vimeo 255532403 w=640 h=564\]

**Key Differences:**

- They are supposedly selling it as an educational product
- Input voltage limit of 2V
- Output voltage limit of 10V
- I didn't have an FPGA in mind (probably because I hadn't used them for anything more than implementing some basic logic gates)
- comes at a whopping $5000 excluding tax and duties

**Ahha plenty of things to work on!  :P**
